Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$11.3B

Record chip sales, softer licensing

QCT chip revenue rose 13% to $9.82B — handsets $6.96B (+14%), automotive $1.05B (+17%) and IoT $1.81B (+7%). Licensing (QTL) went the other way, down 7% to $1.41B.

−$3.1B

A loss on paper only

Operating income actually rose 13% to $2.92B. The $5.7B non-cash tax charge — a valuation allowance on deferred tax assets after new U.S. tax law — pushed GAAP to a $3.12B net loss. Non-GAAP net income was $3.26B, up 7%.

13–14%

A lower tax rate ahead

Qualcomm now expects its effective tax rate to stay at 13–14% with lower cash tax payments in future periods, and guided Q1 FY26 revenue of $11.8–12.6B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $5.7B non-cash tax charge ($5.29 per share) to set up a valuation allowance against U.S. federal deferred tax assets, after the One Big Beautiful Bill Act made Qualcomm expect to pay the 15% corporate alternative minimum tax from fiscal 2026. It is excluded from non-GAAP results; without it, GAAP net income would have been about $2.6B.
  • Other costs of $39M were restructuring and restructuring-related charges (fiscal 2025's other expenses consisted of restructuring charges, per the 10-K).
