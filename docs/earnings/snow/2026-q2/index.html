Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+32%

Product growth stayed above 30%

Product revenue rose 32% to $1.09B, the net revenue retention rate was 125%, and remaining performance obligations — work customers have already contracted — grew 33% to $6.9B. Snowflake also guided Q3 product revenue to $1,125–$1,130M, up 25–26%.

-$340M

Still a GAAP loss, mostly stock pay

The GAAP operating loss was $340M. The main reason: $436M of stock-based compensation-related charges counted as costs this quarter. Leaving those and similar items out, non-GAAP operating income was $128M, an 11% margin.

654

Big customers kept spending

Snowflake ended the quarter with 654 customers spending over $1M a year on the platform (+30% YoY) and 751 Forbes Global 2000 customers. Operating cash flow was $75M and adjusted free cash flow was $68M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $2.1M impairment charge for exiting Snowflake's San Mateo office — part of a $108.6M charge for the first half of FY26, mostly booked in Q1 — excluded from non-GAAP results along with stock-based pay and other items.
