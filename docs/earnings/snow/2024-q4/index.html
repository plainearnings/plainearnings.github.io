Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+33%

Product growth stayed strong

Product revenue rose 33% to $738.1M, the net revenue retention rate was 131%, and remaining performance obligations — work customers have already contracted — grew 41% to $5.2B. Snowflake guided Q1 FY25 product revenue to $745–$750M, up 26–27%, at a 3% non-GAAP operating margin, and full-year FY25 product revenue to $3,250M, up 22%.

-$275.5M

Still a GAAP loss, mostly stock pay

The GAAP operating loss was $275.5M, wider than the $239.8M loss a year ago. The main reason: $320.3M of stock-based compensation-related charges, including about $11.0M of employer payroll taxes on employee stock transactions. Leaving those and similar items out, non-GAAP operating income was $71.0M, a 9% margin (up from 6% a year ago).

461

Big customers kept spending

Snowflake ended the quarter with 461 customers spending over $1M a year on the platform (+39% YoY) and 691 Forbes Global 2000 customers (+8% YoY). Operating cash flow was $344.6M and adjusted free cash flow was $324.3M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $320.3M of stock-based compensation-related charges (including about $11.0M of employer payroll taxes on employee stock transactions), excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • $23.4M of amortization of acquired intangibles, also excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • $2.8M of expenses associated with acquisitions and strategic investments, also excluded from non-GAAP results.
