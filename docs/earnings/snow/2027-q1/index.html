Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+34%

Product growth hit an inflection point

Product revenue rose 34% to $1,334M — the strongest sequential dollar growth in Snowflake's history — and management raised full-year product revenue guidance to $5,840M, up 31%.

$402M

Stock pay is the loss, on paper

Stock-based compensation was $402M in the quarter — larger than the entire $296M net loss. It is a real cost to shareholders through dilution, but it is not cash out the door: adjusted free cash flow was $266M.

126%

Customers keep expanding

Net revenue retention was 126% — existing customers spent 26% more than a year ago — and 779 customers now spend over $1M a year on the platform, up 29%. Remaining performance obligations rose 38% to $9.21B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Stock-based compensation of $402M (cash-flow statement figure) sits inside every cost line and is the main reason GAAP results show a loss while non-GAAP results show a profit.
  • General and administrative costs fell 39% YoY to $129M — the year-ago quarter's $210M was unusually heavy, which flatters the comparison.
