Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+39%

Record growth, messy profit

Revenue grew 39% to $3.44B, Next-Gen Security ARR 66% to $9.73B, and remaining performance obligations 35% to $21.0B — and management raised full-year guidance materially. But GAAP operating profit fell 69%, and the tax line, not the business, produced the net-income growth.

$282M

CyberArk is now the story

Amortization of acquired intangibles jumped 7× to $282M, a $141M fair-value loss on CyberArk's convertible notes hit other income, and $95M of acquisition payments went out in cash. The test is whether CyberArk's identity-security revenue grows fast enough to justify the charges.

$1.9B

Cash generation is the quiet strength

Adjusted free cash flow of $1.94B is 56% of revenue — even in a seasonally slow Q1, the subscription model throws off cash that funds the acquisition strategy without raising money.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A one-time $457M tax benefit — a change in tax position on a historical transaction from the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act transition period — turned $25M of pre-tax income into $482M of net income. Non-GAAP results exclude it through a $705M income-tax adjustment.
  • A $141M fair-value loss on convertible senior notes and capped calls acquired from CyberArk sits inside other expense; it is excluded from non-GAAP results and will swing with the share price.
  • Share-based compensation of $622M and amortization of acquired intangibles of $282M (vs $39M a year ago) are excluded from non-GAAP operating income — but stock pay dilutes shareholders, and the amortization will recur for years.
  • On the same day, Palo Alto signed a definitive agreement to acquire Applied Compute for $4.5B in Palo Alto stock (per the 10-Q; no price was in the earnings release). It is not in this quarter's numbers.
