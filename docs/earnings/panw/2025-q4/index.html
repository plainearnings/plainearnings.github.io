Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+32%

The new platforms are compounding

Next-Generation Security ARR — subscriptions to its newer software platforms — grew 32% to $5.6B, and remaining performance obligations, contracted revenue not yet delivered, grew 24% to $15.8B, more than 6× this quarter's sales. Management called Palo Alto a 'Rule-of-50' company for the fifth straight year.

$338M

The tax line ate the profit

Operating profit more than doubled to $497M, but the tax provision jumped to $338M — a 57% effective rate — after the One Big Beautiful Bill Act forced a one-time $219M deferred-tax remeasurement. GAAP net income fell 29% to $254M even as non-GAAP profit rose 29% to $673M.

$9.2B

Fiscal 2025 in one line

Full-year revenue reached $9.22B, up 15%, with $3.72B of operating cash flow, and the company said it exited the year past a $10B revenue run-rate. GAAP net income for the year was $1.13B, down from $2.58B only because fiscal 2024 included a $1.6B one-time tax benefit.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A one-time deferred tax provision of $218.5M from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (enacted July 4, 2025) — a remeasurement of foreign deferred tax assets — is the main reason the tax provision hit $338M, a 57% effective rate. Non-GAAP results exclude it through a $148.1M income-tax adjustment.
  • A $141.8M net acquisition-related credit — mostly a gain from revaluing the contingent consideration owed on the IBM QRadar assets after the payment terms were amended in June 2025 — sits inside G&A and is why that line fell to $27M from $140M a year ago.
  • Share-based compensation of $372.7M and amortization of acquired intangibles of $36.9M are excluded from non-GAAP results — but stock pay dilutes shareholders, and it is the biggest reason GAAP profit ($254M) trails non-GAAP profit ($673M).
  • On July 30, 2025 Palo Alto signed a definitive agreement to acquire CyberArk for about $25B in cash and stock ($45.00 cash plus 2.2005 Palo Alto shares per CyberArk share). It is not in this quarter's numbers and is expected to close in the second half of fiscal 2026.
