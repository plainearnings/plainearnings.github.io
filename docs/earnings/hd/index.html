Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+2.2%

Same-store growth is ticket-led, not traffic-led

Comparable sales rose 2.2% with the average ticket up 3.0% while comparable transactions fell 0.5% — bigger baskets, not more baskets. That's fine while ticket growth holds, but leaves little cushion if big-ticket demand cools.

−0.6 pp

The margin squeeze is in the goods, not the stores

Gross margin fell 0.6 points to 32.8% because cost of sales (+6.0%) outran sales (+4.9%); SG&A actually grew slower than sales. Watch whether supplier costs keep outpacing prices, or whether Home Depot passes them through.

+1.5%

The full-year bar is modest

Updated guidance implies only about 1.5% diluted EPS growth for fiscal 2026 (2.0% adjusted) — the year is about holding margin, not a second-half acceleration. Nine-month free cash flow of $12.8B funds the dividend and the ~$2.2B of net interest with room to spare.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Adjusted results exclude $182M of amortization from acquired intangibles (mostly SRS) — on that basis EPS was $3.86 instead of the GAAP $3.72. It is a recurring accounting charge, not a one-off.
  • No quarterly cash flow: the release's cash-flow statement covers the nine-month period only, so Q3 operating cash flow, capex and free cash flow cannot be stated — the $12,817M free cash flow is a nine-month figure.
  • Acquisitions are in the comparison: SRS locations (1,320+) are now part of the store base, so part of the year-over-year sales growth reflects businesses that weren't in the base a year ago.
