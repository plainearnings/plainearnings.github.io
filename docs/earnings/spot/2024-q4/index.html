Spotify — Q4 2024 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Q4 2024 · Quarter ended 2024-12-31
Reported 2025-02-04

Spotify grew revenue 16% to €4.24B (about $4.4B) and added a record 35 million monthly users, with operating income hitting a record €477M — capping its first full year of operating profit — and net income swinging to €367M from a €70M loss a year ago.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+35M

Record user growth

Monthly active users grew 12% to 675 million — 35 million net additions, the largest Q4 in Spotify's history and 10 million above guidance. Premium subscribers rose 11% to 263 million, with 11 million net additions, 3 million above guidance. Ad-supported users grew 12% to 425 million.

32.2%

Margins hit record highs

Gross margin hit a record 32.2%, up 555 bps YoY. Premium gross margin reached 34.7% on audiobooks and music, and Ad-Supported gross margin rose to 15.1%. Operating income hit a record €477M, an 11.2% margin, versus a €75M loss a year ago — driving Spotify's first full year of operating profit (€1,365M).

€877M

Cash piled up

Free cash flow hit a record €877M, up 121% YoY, bringing full-year 2024 free cash flow to €2.3B and the cash pile to €7.5B. Net income swung to €367M from a €70M loss a year ago, helped by finance items swinging to a €22M net gain from a €56M net cost. Guidance for Q1 2025: 678M users, 265M subscribers, €4.2B revenue and €548M operating income.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Operating expenses included €96M of 'social charges' — Swedish payroll taxes on share-based pay that swing with Spotify's share price — versus €33M a year ago, and the charges came in €80M above forecast as the share price rose, which is why operating income of €477M landed slightly below the €481M guidance.
  • Cost of revenue included €25M of real-estate impairment charges (€43M for the full year), which partly offset the Ad-Supported segment's gross-margin gains.
  • The 16% YoY drop in operating expenses flatters the comparison: a year ago the quarter carried efficiency charges from the late-2023 workforce restructuring, and lapping them contributed roughly 1,300 bps of the decline. Underneath that, personnel costs and marketing spend both fell.
  • The quarter carried a €132M income tax expense on €499M of pre-tax income — a 26.5% effective tax rate — versus a €61M tax benefit a year ago, when Spotify lost money.
