Spotify — Q2 2025 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Q2 2025 · Quarter ended 2025-06-30
Reported 2025-07-29

Spotify grew revenue 10% to €4.2B (about $4.9B) and added 8 million paying subscribers, but a €421M paper loss on its Exchangeable Notes — the notes are worth more when the share price rises — swung the quarter to a net loss of €86M despite operating income of €406M.

Spotify lost about $2.05 of every $100 of revenue this quarter — operating profit of $9.68 was wiped out by an $8.54 net finance loss (mostly a paper loss on the Exchangeable Notes) and $3.20 of tax. Rows may not add exactly due to rounding.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+12%

Subscribers keep compounding

Premium subscribers grew 12% to 276 million and monthly active users rose 11% to 696 million — 18 million new listeners in the quarter, well above the 11 million Spotify guided. Premium revenue grew 12% to €3,740M on subscriber gains and price rises.

31.5%

Margins widened again

Gross margin rose to 31.5%, up 227 bps YoY. Premium gross margin reached 33.1% as revenue outpaced music costs, and Ad-Supported gross margin jumped to 18.3% on better podcast and music contribution.

−€86M

A paper loss turned profit into a loss

Operating income rose 53% to €406M, but finance costs of €447M — mostly a €421M paper loss on the Exchangeable Notes as Spotify's share price climbed — plus a €134M tax bill on just €48M of pre-tax income left a net loss of €86M. Cash told a different story: free cash flow hit €700M, up 43%.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Operating expenses included €116M of 'social charges' — Swedish payroll taxes on share-based pay that swing with Spotify's share price; €115M of it was tied to share-based compensation, versus €58M a year ago, and the charges came in €98M above forecast as the share price rose.
  • Finance costs included a €421M paper loss on the Exchangeable Notes — the notes are worth more when Spotify's share price rises, which books as a cost, and it reverses if the share price falls. A year ago the same line was a €43M loss.
  • The quarter carried a €134M income tax expense on just €48M of pre-tax income — a 278.7% effective tax rate, versus a €4M tax benefit a year ago. The rate was driven mainly by €106M of tax on the non-deductible Exchangeable Notes loss, plus a €10M deferred-tax charge and a €7M accrual for uncertain tax positions.
