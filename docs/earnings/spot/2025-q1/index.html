Spotify — Q1 2025 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Q1 2025 · Quarter ended 2025-03-31
Reported 2025-04-29

Spotify grew revenue 15% to €4.19B (about $4.5B) and added 5 million paying subscribers, with operating income more than tripling to a record €509M — but a €180M paper loss on its Exchangeable Notes and a €103M tax bill held net income to €225M.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+12%

Subscribers keep compounding

Premium subscribers grew 12% to 268 million and monthly active users rose 10% to 678 million — 5 million new paying subscribers in the quarter, 3 million above Spotify's guidance. Premium revenue grew 16% to €3,771M on subscriber gains and price rises.

31.6%

Margins widened again

Gross margin rose to 31.6%, a Q1 high and up 403 bps YoY. Premium gross margin reached 33.5% on audiobooks and music, and Ad-Supported gross margin jumped to 15.3% on better podcast and music contribution. Operating income more than tripled to a record €509M, a 12.1% margin.

€225M

Paper losses shrank the bottom line

Operating income of €509M was cut by finance costs of €252M — mostly a €180M paper loss on the Exchangeable Notes as Spotify's share price climbed, plus €58M of foreign-exchange losses — and a €103M tax bill, leaving net income of €225M, up 14% YoY. Cash told a stronger story: free cash flow hit a record Q1 €534M, up 158%.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Operating expenses included €76M of 'social charges' — Swedish payroll taxes on share-based pay that swing with Spotify's share price; €75M of it was tied to share-based compensation, versus €82M a year ago, and the charges came in €58M above forecast as the share price rose.
  • Finance costs included a €180M paper loss on the Exchangeable Notes — the notes are worth more when Spotify's share price rises, which books as a cost, and it reverses if the share price falls. A year ago the same line was a €35M loss.
  • The quarter carried a €103M income tax expense on €328M of pre-tax income — a 31.3% effective tax rate, versus a €23M tax benefit a year ago. The rate was driven mainly by €47M of tax on the non-deductible Exchangeable Notes loss, partly offset by a €22M deferred tax benefit and an €8M benefit from income earned in lower-tax jurisdictions.
