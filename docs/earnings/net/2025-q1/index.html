Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+27%

Growth held at 27% with a record deal

Revenue hit $479.1M, up 27% YoY from $378.6M a year ago. Cloudflare signed the largest contract in its history — a more than $100M deal driven by its Workers developer platform — plus its longest-term SASE contract to date, and posted the highest year-over-year growth in net new ACV in three years. Remaining performance obligations — work customers have already committed to — stood at $1,864.2M, with 66% expected within 12 months.

-$38.5M

A narrower operating loss, still GAAP-unprofitable

GAAP loss from operations narrowed slightly to $53.2M, or 11.1% of revenue, versus a $54.6M loss a year ago, as operating expenses grew 20% — slower than revenue. But $16.5M of non-operating income, mostly $21.4M of interest income, narrowed the net loss to $38.5M versus $35.5M a year ago. Excluding $105.9M of stock-based compensation and other items, non-GAAP operating income was $56.0M. Free cash flow jumped to $52.9M, 11% of revenue, from $35.6M a year ago.

111%

Existing customers keep spending more

Dollar-based net retention was 111%, meaning existing paying customers spent 11% more than they did a year earlier. Large customers — those paying over $100,000 a year — rose to 3,527 from 2,878, and paying customers overall grew 27% to 250,819.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Stock-based compensation and related employer payroll taxes totaled $105.895M in Q1 across cost of revenue and operating expenses — the main reason GAAP shows an operating loss while non-GAAP operating income was $56.001M.
  • Amortization of acquired intangible assets was $3.241M in Q1 — also excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • Acquisition-related and other expenses were $0.112M in Q1 — excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • No one-time compensation charge landed in Q1 2025: the $15.000M one-time compensation charge in the year-ago figures dates to the first quarter of 2024.
