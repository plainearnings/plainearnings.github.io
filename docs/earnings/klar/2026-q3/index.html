Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$371M

It keeps much more from each purchase than a year ago

Transaction margin — what Klarna keeps after paying to process, fund and cover losses on each purchase — rose 32% to $371M, or 36.8% of revenue, up 5.7 points. Fair Financing, its highest-earning product per dollar of volume, was also its fastest growing, up 61%.

+500%

Subscriptions are becoming a real business

Subscription revenue rose more than 500% to $43M in the quarter — $178M a year at September's run rate — as paying Klarna Card subscribers tripled to 2.6 million. Card volume more than doubled to $4.5B, and card users shop with Klarna about three times as often.

$(65)M

Still loss-making on the official books

The operating loss was $53M and the net loss $65M, both $30M better than a year ago. Share-based payments jumped 38% to $54M on the timing of the annual compensation cycle; strip those and depreciation out and adjusted operating income was +$21M, up $35M year over year.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Share-based payments were $54M, up 38% year over year, on the timing of Klarna's annual compensation cycle — the company says they should return to pre-Q3 levels. They are the main reason adjusted operating income (+$21M) is $74M above the reported operating loss ($(53)M), along with $22M of depreciation and amortization, partly offset by a $2M restructuring gain.
  • Gains on consumer receivables of $53M are new this quarter (nil a year ago) — they come from Klarna's forward-flow loan-sale programs, which sold about $7.7B of loans in the quarter.
