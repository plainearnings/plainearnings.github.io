Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+16.0%

The stores earned more — the headline didn't show it

Operating income rose 16.0% to $7.8B (5.5% on an adjusted constant-currency basis) and Walmart raised full-year guidance. Net income fell only because of the investment swing below the operating line — judge this quarter on operating income, not EPS.

−$341M

A $2.4B swing in investments explains the profit drop

Walmart booked a $341M net loss on its equity and other investments this quarter, vs a $2,081M gain a year ago. Adjusted EPS, which strips that out, actually grew 4.8%, to $0.65 from $0.62.

+17%

eCommerce and advertising are the growth engine

Global eCommerce sales grew 17% (U.S. +23%), global advertising 25% (U.S. +37%), and membership fees 17%. Guidance raised for FY27: net sales +4.9–5.2% and adjusted operating income +8.0–8.6% in constant currency, adjusted EPS $2.84–$2.89.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $341M net loss on equity and other investments sits below operating income, vs a $2,081M gain a year ago — a $2.4B swing. Adjusted EPS of $0.65 excludes this $0.03-per-share drag. These are mark-to-market moves on Walmart's strategic stakes, not store operations.
  • Last year's International operating income carried a $722M non-cash share-based compensation charge at PhonePe (share plans modified ahead of a potential IPO), which makes this quarter's +132.6% International operating-income growth look far stronger than the underlying +7.0% adjusted constant-currency growth.
  • Last year's quarter also included a $155M reversal of a previously accrued legal charge in Corporate and support; there is no equivalent adjustment this quarter.
  • Flipkart's Big Billion Days sale shifted into Q4, costing International about 500 bps of net-sales growth this quarter; the offsetting benefit is expected in Q4 FY27.
