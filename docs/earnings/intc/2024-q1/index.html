Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$(0.4)B

The loss shrank by 86%

Revenue rose 9% to $12.7B and gross margin widened to 41.0% from 34.2% a year ago, so the net loss attributable to Intel narrowed to $381M from $2,758M. On a non-GAAP basis — excluding share-based pay, restructuring and investment gains — Intel earned $759M, or $0.18 per share, above its guidance.

$(2.5)B

Foundry lost more than the product groups earned

Intel Foundry lost $2,474M on $4,369M of segment revenue, and only $27M of that revenue came from outside customers — down 77% YoY — the rest was wafers sold to Intel's own product groups. The product groups earned $3,311M combined: Client Computing $2,645M, Data Center and AI $482M and Network and Edge $184M.

$(1.2)B

Cash flow went backwards

Operations used $1,223M of cash in the quarter while Intel spent $5,970M on factories and equipment, leaving adjusted free cash flow of $(6,178)M. Intel paid $529M in dividends and guided Q2 to $12.5–13.5B of revenue with GAAP EPS of $(0.05).

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $348M of restructuring and other charges, up from $64M a year ago; excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • A $205M gain on equity investments, net, versus $169M a year ago — mark-to-market moves on Intel's investment portfolio rather than chip sales; excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • A $39M gain from divestiture — deferred consideration on the NAND sale to SK hynix; excluded from non-GAAP results.
