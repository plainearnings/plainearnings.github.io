Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$(1.6)B

The loss, and the reset it triggered

Revenue fell 1% to $12.8B and the operating loss widened to $1,964M from $1,016M a year ago, after a $943M restructuring and litigation charge. Excluding that charge and other adjustments, non-GAAP operating income was just $24M and non-GAAP EPS was $0.02 versus GAAP EPS of $(0.38). Intel answered with a $10B cost-reduction plan, a headcount cut of more than 15%, and a suspended dividend from Q4.

$(2.8)B

Foundry lost more than the product groups earned

Intel Foundry lost $2,830M on $4,320M of segment revenue, and only $77M of that revenue came from outside customers — down 67% YoY — the rest was wafers sold to Intel's own product groups. The product groups still earned $2,912M combined: Client Computing $2,497M, Data Center and AI $276M and Network and Edge $139M.

$8.2B

Cash flow flattered by a factory deal

Operating cash flow was $2,292M, but adjusted free cash flow of $8,155M was lifted by $5,863M of net partner contributions and incentives, mostly the Apollo joint venture for Fab 34 in Ireland. Intel paid $0.5B in dividends — its last before the suspension — and Q3 guidance is $12.5–13.5B of revenue with GAAP EPS of $(0.24).

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $943M of restructuring and other charges — $165M of employee severance and benefit arrangements and $778M of litigation charges and other, including a $780M charge arising out of the R2 litigation; excluded from non-GAAP results. A year ago the same line was $200M.
  • A $(120)M loss on equity investments, net, versus a $(24)M loss a year ago — mark-to-market moves on Intel's investment portfolio rather than chip sales; excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • A $39M gain from divestiture — deferred consideration on the NAND sale to SK hynix; excluded from non-GAAP results.
