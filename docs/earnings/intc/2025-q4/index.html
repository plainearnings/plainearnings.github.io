Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-$333M

A tax bill bigger than the profit

Intel earned $580M from operations on $13,674M of revenue, but a $671M tax provision — a 198.5% rate on $338M of pre-tax income — turned that into a $333M net loss, and $591M attributable to Intel after $258M went to non-controlling interests. On a non-GAAP basis at a 12% tax rate, Intel earned $767M, or $0.15 a share.

+9%

Data centers grew while PCs shrank

Data Center and AI revenue rose 9% to $4,737M with $1,250M of segment operating income, up from $374M a year ago. Client Computing fell 7% to $8,193M, though it still earned $2,209M — the PC business remains Intel's profit engine while servers provide the growth.

-$2.5B

Foundry still loses money — and mostly sells to Intel

Intel Foundry booked $4,507M of revenue but lost $2,509M. Only $222M came from outside customers (10-K: $307M for the full year less $85M in the first nine months). The rest is Intel charging itself, which is why $4,337M of intersegment sales is eliminated again in consolidation.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Restructuring and other charges were a $30M net credit this quarter — a benefit from reducing the previously accrued European Commission fine recorded in 2023 — versus a $57M charge a year ago. For the full year, restructuring and other charges were $2,191M, mostly severance and asset impairments under the 2025 Restructuring Plan.
  • A $109M mark-to-market loss on Escrowed Shares — Intel shares held in escrow for the U.S. Department of Commerce under the CHIPS Act Secure Enclave agreement — sits inside interest and other, net; Intel excludes it from non-GAAP results. The full-year mark-to-market loss was $1,796M.
  • Gains (losses) on equity investments, net were a $97M loss, mostly mark-to-market and price adjustments on Intel's investment portfolio rather than chip sales; also excluded from non-GAAP results.
