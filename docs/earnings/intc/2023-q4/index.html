Intel — Q4 2023 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Intel swung back to profit in the fourth quarter — $2.7 billion attributable to Intel on $15.4 billion of revenue — as PC chip sales jumped 33%, though a $1.1 billion one-off benefit flattered the result and the next quarter is guided to a loss.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$2.7B

Back in profit

Net income attributable to Intel was $2,669M versus a $(664)M loss a year ago, on revenue of $15,406M, up 10%. Gross margin widened to 45.7% from 39.2%, and GAAP EPS was $0.63 versus $(0.16) a year ago.

+33%

PC chips did the heavy lifting

Client Computing revenue jumped 33% to $8,844M and its operating income rose to $2,888M from $524M. Data Center and AI fell 10% to $3,985M and Network and Edge fell 24% to $1,471M, so the recovery was a PC story, not a data-center one.

$(0.25)

A one-off benefit, and a loss guided next

The restructuring line was a $1,142M benefit — VLSI litigation developments, an EC fine and a fee for ending the Tower deal — which flattered operating income. Intel also stretched machinery lives in 2023, lifting gross margin by about $2.5B for the year, and guided Q1 2024 to $12.2–13.2B of revenue with GAAP EPS of $(0.25).

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $1,142M benefit in restructuring and other charges — a negative charge in the quarter, driven by developments in the VLSI litigation, an EC-imposed fine and a fee for ending the Tower Semiconductor acquisition; excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • An $86M gain on equity investments, net, versus $186M a year ago — mark-to-market moves on Intel's investment portfolio rather than chip sales; excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • A $39M gain from divestiture — deferred consideration on the NAND sale to SK hynix; excluded from non-GAAP results.
