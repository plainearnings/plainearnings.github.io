Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+78%

Data centers are carrying Intel

Data Center and AI revenue hit $7,318M, up 78%, with $3,039M of segment operating income. Client computing fell 5% to $8,097M — the growth engine has clearly shifted to Xeon servers for AI data centers.

-$1.9B

Foundry still loses money — and mostly sells to Intel

Intel Foundry booked $6,697M of revenue but lost $1,853M. Only $411M came from outside customers (10-Q); the rest is Intel charging itself, which is why $6,291M is eliminated again in consolidation.

$1.0B

Paper gains flatter the headline — and explain the YoY drop

A $1,005M escrow-share gain plus $330M of equity-investment gains lifted pre-tax profit, which is why GAAP profit attributable to Intel ($3,320M) tops the non-GAAP figure ($2,756M). Headline profit still fell from $4,063M a year ago, when a $5,546M gain on the Altera sale swamped a much weaker operating result.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $1,005M mark-to-market gain on Escrowed Shares — Intel shares held in escrow for the U.S. Department of Commerce under the CHIPS Act Secure Enclave agreement — sits inside interest and other, net. It is a paper gain, and Intel excludes it from non-GAAP results.
  • Gains on equity investments, net were $330M, mostly mark-to-market and price adjustments on Intel's investment portfolio rather than chip sales; also excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • Restructuring and other charges of $54M are a separate operating-expense line this quarter, mostly severance under the 2025 Restructuring Plan.
