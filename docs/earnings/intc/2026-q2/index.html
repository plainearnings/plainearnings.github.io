Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+25%

The core business is growing again

Revenue rose 25% to $16,128M — Intel's strongest growth in over fifteen years — with Data Center and AI up 59% to $6,262M and client computing up 13%. Gross margin jumped to 40.4% from 27.5% a year ago, and operating income was $1,796M versus a $3,176M loss a year ago.

$12.5B

A paper charge created the headline loss

The $12,529M mark-to-market loss on the U.S. government Escrowed Shares is non-cash: Intel's rising share price increased the value of shares it owes the government under the CHIPS Act agreement. Excluding it and other items, non-GAAP net income attributable to Intel was a profit of about $2.2B (EPS $0.42).

$7.0B

Cash flow stayed strong

Intel generated $7.0B of cash from operations in the quarter despite the paper loss. For Q3 it guided revenue of $15.8B to $16.8B, while flagging heavier investment in equipment, clean-room space and substrates to support growth.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Mark-to-market loss on Escrowed Shares: $12,529M, inside 'interest and other, net' — a non-cash charge that rises when Intel's share price rises; Intel excludes it from non-GAAP results.
  • Restructuring and other charges were $170M in the quarter, mostly severance under the 2025 Restructuring Plan (versus $1,890M a year ago).
