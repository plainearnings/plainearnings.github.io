Intel — Q2 2025 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Q2 2025 · Quarter ended 2025-06-28
Reported 2025-07-24

Intel lost $2.9 billion in the second quarter as $1.9 billion of restructuring charges and roughly $1 billion of impairments and one-time costs pushed its costs past flat revenue of $12.9 billion, and its chip factories alone lost $3.2 billion.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-$2.9B

A loss driven by restructuring, not sales

Revenue was flat at $12.9B, so the loss came from costs, not sales: $1,890M of restructuring charges (mostly severance for a ~15% workforce cut) plus about $800M of impairments and $200M of one-time period costs. GAAP EPS was $(0.67); non-GAAP EPS was $(0.10).

-$3.2B

Foundry lost more than the whole company

Intel Foundry lost $3,168M on $4,417M of segment revenue, and only $22M of that revenue came from outside customers — the rest was wafers sold to Intel's own product groups. Client Computing still earned $2,053M and Data Center and AI earned $633M.

-$1.1B

Cash burned while the factories get rebuilt

Adjusted free cash flow was $(1,050)M versus $8,155M a year ago, when partner contributions were $11.4B against $1.3B now. Gross capex was $4.5B. Intel cancelled its planned fabs in Germany and Poland, is consolidating assembly and test in Vietnam and Malaysia, and is slowing construction in Ohio.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $1,890M of restructuring and other charges under Intel's 2025 Restructuring Plan, mostly severance for cutting ~15% of the core workforce; excluded from non-GAAP results and a $(0.45) hit to GAAP EPS.
  • About $800M of non-cash impairment and accelerated depreciation on excess tools with no identified re-use, plus about $200M of one-time period costs — both sat in cost of sales, cut gross margin by about 800 basis points, and hit GAAP and non-GAAP EPS by about $(0.23) and $(0.20).
  • A $502M gain on equity investments, net, versus a $(120)M loss a year ago — mark-to-market moves on Intel's investment portfolio rather than chip sales.
