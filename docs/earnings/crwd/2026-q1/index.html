CrowdStrike — Q1 FY27 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Q1 FY27 · Quarter ended 2026-04-30
Reported 2026-06-03

CrowdStrike grew revenue 26% to $1.39B and signed a record $256M of net new subscriptions, but the core business still lost $31M — $318M of stock pay ate the operating profit — and investment gains plus interest on its cash pile flipped the quarter to a $46M profit, only $28M of which belonged to CrowdStrike shareholders.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$256M

Record new business

Net new ARR — new subscription value signed in the quarter — hit a record $256M, up 32%, taking total ARR to $5.51B (+24% YoY). Management raised full-year net new ARR growth guidance by 520 basis points to 27.7% at the midpoint. Adoption keeps deepening: 51% of customers now use six or more Falcon modules.

-$31M

Operations still lose money on a GAAP basis

The GAAP operating loss was $30.6M — much better than the $118.7M loss a year ago, but still a loss. Stock-based compensation of $317.6M is the whole gap: excluding it, operating income was $325.7M, a 24% margin. The profit is real; shareholders pay for it in dilution.

$469M

Cash flow is the real story

Operating cash flow of $590.9M and free cash flow of $468.5M were both Q1 records — customers pay subscriptions upfront, so cash arrives long before the revenue is counted. Cash and equivalents still ended at $4.55B even after $881M spent on two acquisitions and $176M of buybacks, and the board approved a four-for-one stock split.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Strategic investment gains, $36.4M: realized gains on investments held by the Falcon Funds, booked in other income. They are what turned an operating loss into a profit — but $18.2M of the gain belonged to outside investors in those funds (non-controlling interest), so only $27.8M of the $46.0M net income was attributable to CrowdStrike (GAAP EPS $0.11).
  • Share-based compensation and related employer payroll taxes, $317.6M (up from $265.6M a year ago): the largest non-GAAP add-back and the entire reason GAAP operating income is negative while non-GAAP is $325.7M. It is excluded from non-GAAP results, but it is a real cost — it dilutes existing shareholders.
  • Costs associated with the July 19 Incident, $18.1M (vs $39.7M a year ago): legal fees, remediation, sensor testing and related costs from the July 2024 outage, net of insurance receivables. Fading, but still recurring two years on. Excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • Amortization of acquired intangible assets, $12.4M (vs $7.6M a year ago): rising as acquisitions flow in. Excluded from non-GAAP results; it will recur for years as the acquired intangibles amortize.
  • Acquisition-related expenses, $7.6M: transaction and integration costs for SGNL (acquired Feb 20, 2026 for $627.9M cash) and Seraphic (remaining 90.6% acquired Feb 3, 2026). Excluded from non-GAAP results. The two deals added $886.6M of goodwill.
  • Four-for-one stock split: announced with these results; shareholders of record June 25, 2026 receive three extra shares per share held, with trading on a split-adjusted basis from July 2, 2026. It changes the share count, not the business.
