CrowdStrike — Q3 FY27 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Q3 FY27 · Quarter ended 2026-10-31
Reported 2026-11-23

CrowdStrike posted its best quarter ever — record net new ARR of $419 million, record operating and free cash flow, and revenue up 28% — but the GAAP profit is essentially zero ($25 million on $1.58 billion of sales) because $372 million of stock-based compensation eats the entire operating profit, and the day's real news is the $43.9 billion all-stock deal to buy Zscaler.

The striking thing: for every $100 of revenue, CrowdStrike spends $35 on sales and marketing and $28 on R&D — and after all operating expenses, nothing is left. The $1.54 of net income comes entirely from interest on its cash pile, not from operations. On a non-GAAP basis the picture is healthier ($25.61 of operating profit per $100), but $23.48 of that $100 is share-based compensation — real dilution for shareholders, even if it never leaves the bank account as cash. Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$419M

Best quarter ever on the metrics that matter for a SaaS business

Record net new ARR ($419M, +58% YoY), ARR growth accelerating for the fifth straight quarter to 27%, and record operating and free cash flow. The growth engine is speeding up, with Falcon Guardian crossing $100M ARR in its first year and module adoption still deepening.

1.6%

The GAAP profit is five cents short of nothing

CrowdStrike earned $25 million on $1.58 billion of sales — a 1.6% net margin — because $372 million of stock-based compensation consumed the entire operating profit. Non-GAAP operating margin of 25.6% is the real operating picture, but shareholders pay for it in dilution, and SBC grew 30% year-over-year, faster than revenue.

$43.9B

Zscaler changes the company

The $43.9 billion all-stock deal announced the same morning is the biggest acquisition in CrowdStrike's history and pushes it into network security, the largest security TAM. Management promises immediate accretion to free cash flow per share; the test over the next year is whether the combined platform sustains the ARR acceleration — or whether integrating a company nearly half its size distracts from it.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Share-based compensation and related employer payroll taxes, $371.8M (up from $286.1M a year ago): the largest non-GAAP add-back and the entire reason GAAP operating income is ~$0 while non-GAAP is $405.5M. It is excluded from non-GAAP results, but it is a real cost — it dilutes existing shareholders.
  • Costs associated with the July 19 Incident, $11.3M (vs $26.2M a year ago): legal fees, remediation, sensor testing and related costs from the July 2024 outage, net of insurance receivables. Fading, but still recurring two years on. Excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • Amortization of acquired intangible assets, $19.8M (vs $7.8M a year ago): rising as acquisitions (Fiddler, Dreadnode and earlier deals) flow in. Excluded from non-GAAP results; it will recur for years as the acquired intangibles amortize.
  • Acquisition-related expenses, $3.4M: transaction and integration costs. Excluded from non-GAAP results.
  • Zscaler acquisition: announced with these results (all-stock, ~$43.9B, 1.135 CrowdStrike shares per Zscaler share). It is not in this quarter's numbers. A separate 8-K with the merger agreement (Items 1.01/7.01) was filed the same day.
  • Share repurchases: $175.6 million of common stock repurchased in the first nine months of FY27 (per the cash-flow statement); no quarter-specific figure disclosed.
