Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-15.5%

Sales slipped on timing, not demand

Net sales fell to $5.0B from $5.9B a year ago because several large orders arrived only in late September and could not ship — customer configuration upgrades and data-center readiness delays pushed them into Q2. US sales fell 57% while Asia sales more than doubled.

9.3%

Margins thinned further

Gross margin fell to 9.3% from 13.1% a year ago on product and customer mix, plus $42M of tariff costs and $27M more in inventory write-downs — so gross profit fell 40% even though sales fell only 15%.

$168M

Profit fell, but the order book grew

Net income fell to $168M from $424M a year ago and $195M in Q4. Operations used $918M of cash as inventories rose $1.0B to build ahead of the delayed orders, while management pointed to more than $13B in Blackwell Ultra orders and raised full-year FY26 revenue guidance to at least $36B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Stock-based compensation was $89.1M in the quarter — the main gap between GAAP net income ($168M) and non-GAAP net income ($240M, diluted basis).
  • A $12.0M impairment loss on a non-marketable investment, partly offset by a $6.5M mark-to-market gain on a marketable equity security — both inside other income, net.
  • Management raised the full-year FY26 net sales outlook to at least $36B in this release, up from at least $33B reiterated in the October business update.
