Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+27%

Royalties set another record

Royalty revenue rose 27% to a record $737M and license revenue 25% to $505M, taking total revenue to a record $1,242M — Arm's fourth straight billion-dollar quarter. Data center royalties grew by triple digits as Arm-based server chips spread.

$0.21

GAAP profit is much smaller than the headline

GAAP EPS was $0.21 while non-GAAP EPS was $0.43. The gap is mostly $285M of share-based pay plus $28M of related employer taxes — and equity investments added only $6M this quarter, versus $39M a year ago, which is why GAAP net income fell 12% even as operating income rose 6%.

$1.62B

The license pipeline keeps building

Annualized contract value (ACV) — a normalized view of license revenue — rose 28% to $1,620M, and customers have now signed 21 Compute Subsystems (CSS) licenses. For Q4 FY26 Arm guided revenue to about $1.47B (plus or minus $50M).

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Share-based pay is the main gap between the two profit numbers: the non-GAAP reconciliation adds back $285M of equity-settled share-based compensation (including $8M in cost of sales) plus $28M of related employer taxes (net of R&D incentives) and $6M of restructuring costs, which is why GAAP net income of $223M sits far below non-GAAP net income of $457M.
  • Net income got only a $6M lift from equity investments this quarter, versus $39M a year ago — that swing, not the core business, is why GAAP profit fell year on year.
