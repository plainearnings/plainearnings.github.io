Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+20%

Both halves of the business set records

License revenue rose 29% to a record $819M and royalty revenue 11% to $671M — also a record for a fourth quarter. Data center royalties more than doubled as Arm-based server chips spread, and Arm's share of CPU compute at the top hyperscalers is now about 50%.

$0.29

GAAP profit is much smaller than the headline

GAAP EPS was $0.29 while non-GAAP EPS was $0.60. The gap is mostly $261M of share-based pay plus $29M of related employer taxes — and unlike a year ago, when equity investments lost $290M, this quarter they added $20M.

$2B+

The AGI chip is the next growth bet

Customer demand for the Arm AGI CPU, Arm's first own data-center chip, now exceeds $2B across fiscal 2027–28 — more than double what Arm stated at launch, with Meta as lead partner. For Q1 FY27 Arm guided revenue to $1.26B (plus or minus $50M) with non-GAAP EPS of $0.40 (plus or minus $0.04).

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Share-based pay is the main gap between the two profit numbers: the non-GAAP reconciliation adds back $261M of equity-settled share-based compensation (including $7M in cost of sales) plus $29M of related employer taxes (net of R&D incentives), $1M of acquisition-related expenses and $2M of restructuring costs, which is why GAAP net income of $313M sits far below non-GAAP net income of $641M.
  • Net income got a $20M lift from income on equity investments — a swing from a $290M loss a year ago. That is market-driven, not operating profit, and Arm excludes it from its non-GAAP results.
