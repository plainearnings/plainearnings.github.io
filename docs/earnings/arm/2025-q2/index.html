Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$844M

Revenue beat guidance on record royalties

Revenue rose 5% to $844M, above the high end of guidance ($780–830M), on record second-quarter royalty revenue. Royalty revenue grew 23% to $514M while license revenue fell 15% to $330M on the timing of high-value deals — but bookings were strong, ACV grew 13% to $1,253M, and RPO rose 10% sequentially to $2,385M. For the first half of the fiscal year, revenue was $1,783M, up 20% year over year.

+23%

Royalties keep climbing on Armv9

Royalty revenue grew 23% to $514M on wider use of the Armv9 architecture and smartphone market recovery. Armv9 now contributes around 25% of royalty revenue, up from around 10% a year ago, with smartphone application-processor royalties growing strongly on Apple's iPhone 16 and MediaTek's Dimensity 9400. Microsoft's Azure Cobalt 100 and Google's Axion, both Arm-based cloud chips, reached general availability in the quarter.

$107M

A tax benefit did the heavy lifting

GAAP net income was $107M, or $0.10 per share, versus a $0.11 loss a year ago — but operating income was just $64M, a 7.6% margin, as R&D climbed to $507M on a 21% jump in engineering headcount. A $43M tax benefit, including a one-time benefit, plus $29M of net interest income did the heavy lifting. Excluding the equity loss and share-based pay, non-GAAP net income was $317M, or $0.30 per share. Free cash flow was -$65M on working-capital timing.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Arm's $43M tax benefit included a one-time benefit: the non-GAAP tax rate was 5.6% but would have been 13.5% without it — the benefit did much of the work in turning a $64M operating profit into $107M of net income.
  • Arm recorded a $10M loss from equity investments in the quarter, which it strips out of its non-GAAP results: excluding it (and share-based pay), non-GAAP net income was $317M versus $107M GAAP.
