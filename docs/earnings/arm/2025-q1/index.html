Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$939M

Record revenue, above guidance

Revenue rose 39% to a record $939M, above the high end of guidance ($875–925M), on record license revenue of $472M (+72%) as companies signed multiple high-value long-term deals, plus royalty revenue of $467M (+17%). The pipeline looks strong too: remaining performance obligations were $2,168M (+29% YoY) and annualized contract value $1,193M (+14% YoY). Arm guided Q2 FY25 revenue of $780–830M and left full-year guidance unchanged at $3,800–4,100M.

+50%

Armv9 is lifting what Arm earns per chip

Smartphone royalty revenue rose 50% year over year as premium phones adopted Armv9 chips, which carry higher royalty rates. Armv9 now contributes around 25% of royalty revenue, up from around 20% last quarter. Automotive royalties grew on chips with higher rates in driver assistance and digital cockpits, and cloud compute benefited from early adoption of Armv9 server chips, while IoT/embedded declined on continued inventory correction.

$223M

Profit more than doubled, but R&D is eating most of the gross profit

GAAP net income more than doubled to $223M, or $0.21 per share versus $0.10 a year ago, on a 19.4% operating margin — but R&D climbed to $485M on a 23% jump in engineering headcount as Arm invests in Arm Compute Subsystems. Excluding share-based pay and the equity gain, non-GAAP net income was $419M, or $0.40 per share. Free cash flow was -$348M, seasonally the weakest quarter as Arm pays annual bonuses, and a $573M payroll-tax timing benefit from Q4 reversed.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Arm recorded a $24M gain from equity investments in the quarter, which it strips out of its non-GAAP results: excluding it (and share-based pay), non-GAAP net income was $419M versus $223M GAAP.
