Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$824M

Record revenue, above guidance

Revenue rose 14% to a record $824M, above guidance ($720–800M), on record royalty revenue of $470M (+11%) and license revenue of $354M (+18%) from multiple high-value long-term deals signed as companies invest in AI. The pipeline looks strong too: remaining performance obligations were $2,433M (+38% YoY) and annualized contract value $1,160M (+15% YoY, +5% QoQ). Arm guided Q4 FY24 revenue of $850–900M and raised full-year FY24 revenue guidance to $3,155–3,205M (from $2,960–3,080M).

+11%

Armv9 is lifting what Arm earns per chip

Royalty revenue hit a record $470M as Armv9 chips, which carry royalty rates typically at least double Armv8, spread into more markets: Armv9 now contributes around 15% of royalty revenue, up from around 10% last quarter. Smartphone royalties grew strongly on the market recovery and AI-enabled Armv9 handsets, cloud servers grew on market-share gains, and automotive grew on driver-assistance chips (microcontrollers slowed). IoT/embedded was flat on last year's inventory correction. Customers reported shipping 7.7 billion Arm-based chips in the September quarter, taking the cumulative total to 280.3 billion.

$134M

Record revenue, thinner profit

GAAP operating profit fell 45% to $134M (a 16.3% margin) after operating expenses rose 45% to $654M: R&D was $432M (52.4% of revenue) on 20% engineering headcount growth, and total share-based pay was $196M ($8M in cost of sales, $139M in R&D, $49M in SG&A). Non-GAAP operating profit was $338M, up 17%, a 41.0% margin. Net income of $87M (vs $182M a year ago) compares with non-GAAP net income of $305M (+36%). Free cash flow was $251M, and $724M over the trailing twelve months (+63%).
