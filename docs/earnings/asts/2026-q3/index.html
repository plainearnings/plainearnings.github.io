Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+187%

Revenue is ramping, from a tiny base

Revenue hit $42.3M, up from $14.7M a year ago, driven by gateway deliveries and U.S. government milestones. The company stayed on track for $150–200M of revenue for the full year, with upside from contracts signed recently.

$3.2B

A fortress balance sheet buys time

Cash, cash equivalents and restricted cash totalled about $3.2B at quarter-end. On top of that, Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications is providing about $1.0B of non-dilutive funding for midband satellites, and a further ~$1.2B pipeline of U.S. quasi-government financing is in the works for 2027.

100+

The constellation is the whole bet

Production is running through BlueBird 51, with a target of about 45 satellites in orbit by mid-2027 and launch agreements covering more than 100 BlueBirds across 2027–2028. AST also announced Blacklift, its own 20-ton reusable rocket, to launch up to five satellites at a time starting in late 2028.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Stock-based compensation was $52.3M in the quarter — more than revenue. Adjusted operating expenses, which exclude it, depreciation and the conversion loss, were $133.6M.
  • The $0.05M loss on involuntary conversion this quarter compares with a $125.9M loss in Q2 2026, which is why total operating expenses fell from $329.1M to $211.0M quarter over quarter.
