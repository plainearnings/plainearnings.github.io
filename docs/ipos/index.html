How an IPO works

1

The company files

It publishes an S-1 — a long SEC document with its financial history, risks and plans. That's when a company appears on this page as “filed”.

2

It sets a price range

The company and its banks pitch big investors (the “roadshow”), set a price range, and take orders. Strong demand can push the price up; weak demand can delay the deal.

3

It prices and trades

The final price is set the evening before trading starts. From day one it's a normal public company — and within a few months it files its first earnings report, which we'll explain like every other company here.

Plain Earnings is an educational project. Nothing here is financial advice. IPO dates, price ranges and deal sizes come from companies' SEC filings and press reports, and they can change at any time — an IPO can be delayed, resized or withdrawn.
